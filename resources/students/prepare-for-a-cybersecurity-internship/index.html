STUDENT GUIDE

Prepare for a cybersecurity internship

A good internship starts before the first day. Use the public opportunity details as your source of truth, prepare questions about the work, and make your boundaries explicit before you receive access.

For students · Practical guidance · Updated 21 September 2026

Read the opportunity as a brief

Check the domain, type, location, language, listed skills, and application status on the opportunity page. Those fields describe the public record; they do not replace a conversation about your school requirements or the work itself.

\n

Write down what you already know, what you can demonstrate, and what you need to learn. A short portfolio, lab note, or project explanation is more useful than a list of tools without context.

\n
  • Which outcome should the internship produce?
  • Which systems, data, or environments are in scope?
  • Who reviews the work and how do you ask for help?

Prepare for responsible security work

Only test systems when the owner has given clear permission and the scope is written down. Keep notes about the instruction you received, the limits of the work, and where to report a concern.

\n

If a listing or project leaves a safety question unanswered, ask before acting. The DIVD.Works safety guidance is the baseline for respectful, authorised work.

\n
  • Never access, use, or share systems or information without permission.
  • Raise an incident or unsafe situation as soon as possible.
  • Treat a placement as a learning relationship: feedback and questions are part of the work.
